Part 1 & 2: Core Philosophy & Construction

Understanding how scale degrees map directly into playable chord structures.

1

Major & Major 7th

Formula: $1 - 3 - 5 - 7$

The foundation of major harmony. The Major 3rd provides a bright quality, while the Major 7th adds a soft, lush resolution.

Example: $Cmaj7$
Notes: $C - E - G - B$
Voicing: $A$-string Drop-2 or open position.
2

Minor 7th

Formula: $1 - \flat3 - 5 - \flat7$

Flatted 3rd ($\flat3$) creates a somber quality, and the flatted 7th ($\flat7$) relaxes tension for jazz, blues, and pop.

Example: $Dm7$
Notes: $D - F - A - C$
Voicing: Low $E$-string barre or shell shape.
3

Dominant 7th

Formula: $1 - 3 - 5 - \flat7$

Combines a bright Major 3rd with a bluesy Minor 7th ($\flat7$). Creates harmonic instability that wants to resolve to the I chord.

Example: $G7$
Notes: $G - B - D - F$
Voicing: Open cowboy chord or shell shape.

Module 1

The Fretboard Map & Pitch Basics

1. Standard Tuning & String Mnemonics

The standard guitar has 6 strings numbered from highest (thinnest) to lowest (thickest): 1-E, 2-B, 3-G, 4-D, 5-A, 6-E.

Popular Mnemonics (Strings 6 to 1):
Eddie Ate Dynamite, Goodbye Bob Early
Every Amatuer Does Get Better Eventually

2. Frets, Semitones & Tones

  • Half Step (Semitone): The distance of 1 fret on guitar (e.g., Fret 3 to Fret 4).
  • Whole Step (Tone): The distance of 2 frets on guitar (e.g., Fret 3 to Fret 5).

3. The Chromatic Scale & Natural Half Steps

Music uses 12 distinct pitches. Notes progress alphabetically from $A$ to $G$ with sharps ($\sharp$) or flats ($\flat$) in between.

Golden Rule of Natural Half Steps:

There are NO sharps or flats between E & F and B & C. They sit right next to each other on adjacent frets!

A → A♯/B♭ → B → C → C♯/D♭ → D → D♯/E♭ → E → F → F♯/G♭ → G → G♯/A♭ → A

How Chords Are Built (Interval Theory)

Click speaker buttons to audition chord sound qualities.

An interval is the distance between two notes measured relative to the root ($1$). By stacking specific interval combinations, we create unique musical emotions.

Chord Quality Formula Emotional Character Audition Audio Try in Studio

Minor Triad $1 - \flat3 - 5$ Sad, somber, introspective

Module 3

Reading Fretboard Diagrams & Shell Voicings

1. Understanding Fretboard Grids

Fretboard diagrams represent the neck of the guitar looking directly at the front of the instrument:

  • Vertical Lines: Represent frets (Fret 1, 2, 3, etc.).
  • Horizontal Rows: Represent guitar strings. String 1 (High E) is at the top, and String 6 (Low E) is at the bottom.
  • Colored Circle Nodes: Highlight exact finger placement locations.
  • Nut (Left thick bar): Fret 0 (open strings played without touching frets).

2. Why Shell Voicings Are Superpowers

Full 6-string barre chords can sound muddy. Shell Voicings strip a chord down to its 3 essential DNA notes:

The Shell Formula: Root (1) + 3rd + 7th

The 5th is omitted because it only adds weight, while the 3rd defines quality (Major/Minor) and the 7th defines color!
